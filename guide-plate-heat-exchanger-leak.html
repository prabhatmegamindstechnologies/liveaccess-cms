Plate heat exchanger leaks and mixing of media: what to check

A plate heat exchanger can leak to the outside, or leak inside so that product and service media mix. An external leak is visible. An internal leak often shows first as a quality or test result.

By LiveAccess team, TPS · Published 29 October 2026 · Liquid processing

Safety first. Isolate, drain and depressurise both sides and let the unit cool before you loosen the pack. Treat any suspected mixing as a food safety issue under your site's quality procedure.

Symptoms

  • Drips or spray from the plate pack edge.
  • Water in the product, or product in the cooling or heating water.
  • Unexpected conductivity, solids or test results downstream.
  • Leaks that start after a CIP or a temperature change.

Likely causes

  • Gasket wear and attack. Gaskets harden, crack or swell with age, heat and cleaning chemicals.
  • Wrong tightening. A plate pack tightened outside the dimension on the nameplate leaks or crushes gaskets.
  • Pressure shocks. Water hammer, fast valve closing or pump start-up can open the gasket seal.
  • Plate cracks and pinholes. Fatigue, corrosion or chloride attack can make small holes that let media mix.
  • Wrong pressure balance. If service media pressure is higher than product pressure, a hole lets service media into the product.

What to check, in order

  1. Mark where the leak appears on the pack, and at which temperature or pressure it starts.
  2. Measure the plate pack tightening dimension and compare it with the nameplate or service documentation.
  3. Inspect gaskets for cracks, hardness and seating at the next opening.
  4. If you suspect mixing, test for it with the method used at your site, such as a pressure test or dye check.
  5. Check that product-side pressure is kept higher than service-side pressure where your process requires it.
  6. Review recent changes to CIP chemicals, concentrations or temperatures.

When to call a TPS engineer

Call a TPS engineer if you find mixing between media, cracked plates, or a leak that returns after re-gasketing. Product safety may be affected.
